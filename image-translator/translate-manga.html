Translate manga and webtoons right in Chrome

Reading a raw chapter in Japanese, Korean or Chinese? Image Translator reads the speech bubbles — including vertical Japanese text — and draws the translation over them, in a side panel next to the page. It runs on your computer, so chapters are never uploaded anywhere.

How to translate a manga page

  1. Install Image Translator — free, no account.
  2. Open the chapter in Chrome, on any reader site.
  3. Press Alt+Shift+S (⌥⇧S on Mac) and drag a frame over a page or a single panel. Or right-click a page image and choose “Translate image” to take it in full resolution.
  4. Read the translation drawn over the bubbles. Press and hold the picture to see the original; below it, every bubble is listed with its original text.

What it reads

  • Japanese manga — vertical text in speech bubbles, read in the right order: columns from right to left, each from top to bottom.
  • Korean manhwa and webtoons — Hangul in horizontal lines.
  • Chinese manhua — Simplified and Traditional characters.
  • English comics — and 36 languages in total, translated into any of 39.

Tips for better results

  • Select one page or one panel at a time: the bigger the letters in the frame, the better they are read.
  • Long webtoon strips: scroll to the part you are reading and press Alt+Shift+A to translate what is on screen. A whole strip squeezed into one picture makes the letters too small.
  • Reader sites that draw pages on a canvas don't offer “Translate image” in the right-click menu — select the area with Alt+Shift+S (⌥⇧S on Mac) instead; it works on anything visible.
  • Download the language pack once (the panel shows a button for it), and the next chapters translate instantly, even offline.

What it can't do yet

  • Hand-drawn lettering and stylized sound effects are often not recognized.
  • Very small or blurry text in low-resolution scans loses accuracy.
  • Hebrew, Bengali and Kannada text can't be read yet.

Private by design

The pages you read are processed on your computer: text recognition runs on your graphics card, and translation is done by Chrome's built-in on-device translator. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account and no daily limit. How it works offline →

Frequently asked questions

Can it translate raw manga from Japanese to English?

Yes. Select a page or a panel with Alt+Shift+S (⌥⇧S on Mac): the Japanese text, including vertical text in speech bubbles, is read and translated into English — or into any of 39 languages.

Does it work on any manga or webtoon site?

It works with anything Chrome shows: right-click a page image, or select an area of the screen. Only Chrome's own pages, the Chrome Web Store and DRM-protected video can't be captured.

Is it free? Is there a daily limit?

It is free, with no account and no limits: everything runs on your own computer.

Does it work offline?

Yes, once Chrome has downloaded the language pack for your pair — about 80 MB per language, once.
